WIDT Stairs Project

Nine steps in three modules curve through 90 degrees to an 85-inch castle prop. Each regular rise and the final rise onto the castle is 8 1/2 inches. The castle is an independently visible gray cylinder, not a selectable stairway part. Every module has two flat pine 2x4 caster beams, with four casters mounted directly beneath their ends. Modeled casters are 4 1/4 inches high, and beam tops are 5 3/4 inches above the floor. Flat pine 2x4 lower cross-pieces bear directly on the caster beams, spanning 5 3/4 to 7 1/4 inches. There are no plywood base rails. Most pine 1x4 uprights start on those cross-pieces at 7 1/4 inches. Front posts sit flush with the framing front, behind the quarter-inch risers, and bear directly beneath the upper front cross-pieces. Front and side upper supports have complementary 1 3/4-inch half-laps: front supports retain their lower halves and carry the side supports' upper halves. Rear half-laps bear the rear supports on the sides at module backs, and the sides on the rear supports elsewhere. Intermediate side supports stop at the following front posts' faces, without short tongues or matching post notches. In modules 2 and 3, the first left support extends onto a new 1x4 cross-piece mounted against the rear upright faces. Its original rear support butts against it without a left half-lap. The next two left front posts start at the extended support's bottom, with bottom notches bearing on its top. The new cross-pieces have half-inch seats for the upper panel ribs; those ribs remain uncut at this joint. Each upper module has one 8-by-8-inch triangular quarter-inch plywood gusset on the front faces of its left rear upright and rear tread support, within the existing footprint. Front bearing caps and separate upper rear-support cleats are omitted. Each upper module also has an 8-inch-high rectangular quarter-inch plywood rear brace on the rear cross-beam, spanning the rear uprights' outer edges. Its rear face meets the upright fronts; the rear attachment blocks move forward a quarter inch against it. These braces follow Uprights visibility. Pine 2x4 lower attachment blocks have 3 1/2-inch cut lengths and broad faces against the uprights or their rear braces, behind front posts and ahead of rear posts. They normally span 7 1/4 to 10 3/4 inches. Step 2's right block is ripped to 2 1/4 inches tall to clear its spindle mount. Step 1 has two flat pine 1x4 carriers at 5 3/4 to 6 1/2 inches, supporting three fore-and-aft boards ripped to 1 1/2 inches high: a pine 1x3 on the left and pine 2x4s beside and behind the newel. The deep front pine 1x6 spans 2 1/2 to 8 inches, meeting the tread underside and the newel's stopped corner bevel without a newel notch. Half-inch plywood treads have fitted newel and spindle cutouts. Higher risers run between consecutive tread undersides; the lowest riser retains one inch of floor clearance. Curved one-eighth-inch plywood siding covers the outer side. Doubled half-inch bottom ribs are reinforced by square-cut 4-inch pine 2x4 panel support blocks running radially inward. Block tops attach directly beneath existing lower cross-pieces at 5 3/4 inches, and bottoms meet the ribs at 2 1/4 inches. Front blocks use the second-step cross-pieces to clear the front casters; rear blocks use the last steps' front cross-pieces. Module 1 omits its front block. There are no panel support ties. Elevated half-inch plywood panel ribs have a 3 1/2-inch radial width and fit between upright faces: three upper segments in module 2, and three middle plus three upper segments in module 3. Vertical 3 1/2-inch pine 2x2 blocks mount on the uprights' broad faces beneath the rib ends. Steps 4 and 7 instead use their spindle supports at the first upper rib ends, shortening these supports by half an inch. Oak 2x2 spindles mount on blocks behind front right posts and ahead of rear right posts. Steps 3-9 use vertical pine 2x2 supports, normally 8 1/2 inches long; step 2 and rear posts retain 2x4 blocks. Their notch shoulders remain 32 inches above the step-support baseline, with sloping tops two inches higher. The oak 4x4 newel has a stopped front-inner bevel, and separate lower pine cheek blocks support its attachment. Fasteners and connection capacities are not modeled.
