WIDT Stairs Project

Configure parameters

Modules arranged through 90 degrees, without lower front cross-pieces. Above step 1, notched vertical uprights sit at the step edges and support the upper front cross-pieces and back step supports. Step 1 instead has a pine 1x4 left support on edge, with its outer face on the side boundary and its top under the tread. A pine 2x4 newel inner block replaces its right-side 1x4 and extends beneath step 2's right upright. The left support's front notch clears its front cross-piece. Its square-cut rear end butts against step 2's upright, with upper relief clearing its pine 2x4 cross-piece where needed. Every step except the lowest also has a pine right step support on edge, seated three-quarters of an inch into the front upright and into the rear upright where present. Its back end half-laps the back step support, or seats in the rear upright at a module boundary. Except at module backs, a matching left step support follows the shorter inside edge. Both extend to a back step support shifted one quarter inch behind the tread's rear boundary. Side supports retain bottom half-lap notches, matching top notches in the back support. Every module has two pine 2x4 support beams laid flat, extending from its first step to its last. Four casters mount directly beneath their ends, using a modeled caster height of 4.25 inches, with the beam tops 5.75 inches above the floor. Two six-inch-wide, three-quarter-inch plywood base rails sit on the beams and finish at 6.5 inches. Both module-1 beam fronts are mitered against its front cross-piece. Its right beam meets that cross-piece just inside the newel inner block, with both front caster plates clear of the cross-piece. The right front caster is set back an additional inch along its beam. Other modules' square-cut beam fronts reach the footprint boundary; their whole-inch length rounding is trimmed from the rear. Left and right front/rear uprights start at the rail tops, 6.5 inches above the floor. Pine 2x4 support cross-pieces rest on the rails, spanning 6.5 to 8 inches above the floor, and seat in half-inch-deep, bottom-open face notches on every step except step 1. Each module also has a flat pine 2x4 rear support cross-piece between its rear uprights, with matching notches. Combined left uprights share one bottom notch. Right bottom notches are longer, retaining the same eight-inch shoulder elevation. Beam placement respects the footprint and first-step framing; right beams and their casters move inward as needed to clear the remaining material in the extended right uprights. Rear cross-pieces are trimmed clear of the support cross-pieces in front of them. Apart from the newel blocking, there are no caster platforms, reinforcement blocks, linking pieces, or extra step-2 casters. Step 1's lower support cross-piece and back step support are temporarily omitted. With nine steps, a flat pine 1x4 front step support extends to step 1's left tread edge; with eight steps it is a flat pine 2x4. Its front edge meets the riser's back face, its upper face meets the tread underside, and the front cross-piece is lowered beneath it. A matching flat rear step support rests across the rails parallel to the front support, immediately behind the newel's rearmost corner, and ends flush with the tread's right edge and front cross-piece. Its left end reaches the tread edge, mitered clear of step 2's pine support cross-piece. Other step counts omit both pine supports. Step 2's riser ends at step 1's tread underside. All uprights are 1 1/2-inch-thick pine, including module-front, rear, and combined uprights. Module-back rear uprights have upper notches. When the left front and rear uprights meet or overlap, one pine board replaces their union, cut from dressed 2x6, 2x8, or 2x10 stock. Separate uprights are 2x4s, 1 1/2 by 3 1/2 inches. Horizontal 1x4 framing remains 3/4 inch thick. Step 1 has no edge-on 1x4 side supports. There are no caster-platform dados. Top frames have a cross-piece at each end supporting a half-inch-thick tread extending forward to the front of the riser. Tread inner edges follow the castle radius; outer edges follow the siding's inner radius. Step 1 instead has a straight right edge tangent to step 2's outer arc. Treads have fitted cutouts around the newel and banister spindles. Left uprights stay outside the castle's tangent plane, and the left side supports' right faces align with the uprights' right faces. All front uprights have full-height front-edge bevels flush with the quarter-inch risers' back planes, plus parallel deeper notches for the upper front cross-pieces. There are no separate stopped riser-clearance notches. Step 2's rear endpoints move slightly forward to retain 2x4 stock, with adjoining supports refitted to match. Module-last risers fasten directly to the thicker uprights; there are no separate vertical 2x2 riser cleats. Edge-on pine 2x4 support blocks rest on the pine cross-pieces against the uprights' inward faces. Ordinary blocks match the uprights' front-to-back widths. Each module's last step has one continuous block per side, from the riser's back plane to the back of the rear upright, bearing on both pine cross-pieces. Ends are beveled and upper fronts notched where needed to clear the footprint, riser, and framing. The horizontal blocks span 8 to 11.5 inches above the floor. Step 1 has no lower cross-piece or attachment blocks. Curved one-eighth-inch plywood siding covers each module's outer side from one inch above the floor to the tread tops. Its inner face meets the curved tread edges, and it has its own visibility control. Each module has a quarter-inch particle-board back panel, with its bottom at the rear support cross-piece bottom and its top one and a half inches above the back step support bottom. Its sides align with the outside rear upright edges. Particle board is darker brown with a rough, grain-free texture. Oak 2x2 banister spindles attach outside the front right uprights on steps 2 onward and the rear right upright of each module, centered front-to-back on each upright. The siding has cutouts around them. Their sloping tops stay two inches above the adjustable spindle shoulder ascent, initially 32 inches above the step-support baseline, with half-inch side notches. Four-inch-high oak 2x4 spindle shims fill the gaps between modules, with faceted sloping tops meeting the notch shoulders. The oak 4x4 newel is 3 1/2 inches square, with a level bottom one inch above the floor and a level top three inches above the selected shoulder ascent at its back-face center. Its side faces follow the extended right tread edge, with its right face two inches outside it, measured perpendicular to the edge. Its front face passes through the front cross-piece's rear-right corner. The pine 2x6 front cross-piece stays parallel to the tread front and ends flush with the right-edge line. Its back edge is straight and unnotched. The newel's front-inner corner is beveled flush with that face only through the cross-piece's contact height, leaving the post square above the joint. The stopped bevel is 10 degrees in the default nine-step layout. Its top stays at the previous elevation, with its bottom two inches lower. An unnotched pine 2x4 inner block runs against the post's inner face from that cross-piece toward step 2's right upright, with its rear end cut square at the first siding or step-2 spindle obstruction, or at the upright's back if neither intersects. Its front is mitered against the cross-piece, and both newel blocks follow the new right edge. There is no beam block. A pine 2x4 newel rear block runs front-to-back on edge, against the post's back and the inner block's right face, with its bottom at three inches and its top at 6 1/2 inches. Its square rear end stops before step 2's extended right upright, independently of the inner block's rear-end plane. The first tread and flat front support are cut around the post, and step 1's right-side siding has front and rear pieces fitted around the post. Fasteners and connection capacities are not modeled. Separate six-inch vertical pine 2x4 back support blocks attach to the inward faces of each non-first step's front uprights within a module, except step 2. Their front bevels meet the preceding back step support. They are centered on that support, with the smallest vertical adjustment needed to clear existing woodwork. All risers above step 1 are one step rise tall, running between the undersides of consecutive treads with half an inch of tread overlap. Module-last risers no longer extend down to the support beams. A separate stiffening piece is not yet modeled. The lowest riser retains one inch of floor clearance. All regular stair rises share one height, rounded to the nearest quarter inch. The separate transition onto the 77-inch castle top takes the remaining height and can differ. The castle is another stage prop, shown as an optional gray cylinder, not a selectable part of the stairway. Approximate casters sit directly beneath the front and rear of each module's caster beams.
